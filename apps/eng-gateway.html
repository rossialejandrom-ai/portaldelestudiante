Frequency Adverbs

Master how to use and position adverbs of frequency correctly.

📖 Quick Reference

always (100%) → usually (80%) → often (60%) → sometimes (40%) → rarely/seldom (10%) → never (0%)

📍 Position: BEFORE the main verb, but AFTER the verb be

✅ She always eats breakfast.  |  ✅ He is never late.

🔄 Section 4

Present Continuous

Actions happening right now or temporary situations. Don't forget the -ing spelling rules!

📖 Quick Reference

Structure
am/is/are + verb+ing
She is reading.
They are playing.
-ing Spelling Rules
play → playing
make → making (drop e)
run → running (double)
study → studying
State Verbs (NO -ing!)
know · love · hate · think
believe · need · want
understand · like · care

⚡ Section 5

Simple vs Continuous

The hardest part — knowing WHEN to use each tense. This section is key for your exam!

📖 Use Present Simple for…

✔ Regular habits: She goes to school every day.
✔ General truths: Water boils at 100°C.
✔ State verbs (love, know, need…): I love music.

📖 Use Present Continuous for…

✔ Actions happening NOW: She is reading right now.
✔ Temporary situations: He is living in London this year.
⚠ NOT with state verbs: ✗ I am knowing the answer.

🕰️ Section 6

Past Simple

Regular verbs, negatives, questions, and the -ed pronunciation rules.

📖 Quick Reference

Regular: add -ed
play → played
arrive → arrived
plan → planned
study → studied
Negative
subject + didn't + base
She didn't go.
They didn't eat.
Question
Did + subject + base?
Did he arrive?
Did they see it?

-ed pronunciation: /t/ after voiceless sounds (watched, liked) · /d/ after voiced sounds (arrived, called) · /ɪd/ after t or d (wanted, started)
